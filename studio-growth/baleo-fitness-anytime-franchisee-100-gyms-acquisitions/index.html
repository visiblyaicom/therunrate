How Anytime Fitness Franchisee Baleo Reached 100 Gyms by Buying Other Owners' Clubs

Baleo bought about 94% of its gyms from other franchisees. Then it got more members out of the same clubs. If you ever sell your gym, this is what the buyer looks like.

Baleo Fitness runs more than 100 Anytime Fitness gyms in 15 states. Four years ago it had none.

Almost all of that growth came from buying gyms other owners already ran. About 94% of its gyms were bought from other franchisees, including all of its first 40. Co-founders Michael Chere and John Christopher laid out how in an interview with Franchise Times.

For any gym owner, that's worth a minute. If you ever sell, your buyer probably looks a lot like Baleo.

How do franchisees grow to 100 gyms?

At Baleo, mostly by buying. The group bought its first two gyms in Greenville and Spartanburg, South Carolina, at the end of 2022. It finished 2025 with 80. It now plans 25 to 30 more a year, about five new builds and the rest bought. Buying an open gym gets you members, staff and cash coming in on day one. Building one means waiting for all three. Baleo has done it without private equity, using a seed round, money from friends, its own funds and a development line of credit.

Why Anytime? Christopher told Franchise Times the barriers to entry were lower than at the big budget gyms. Smaller gyms mean smaller deals, which suited two first-time franchisees. That choice shaped everything after it. A brand with thousands of small gyms has plenty of owners who might want to sell, and a playbook you can repeat club after club.

Same box, more members

Buying gyms only pays if you run them better than the last owner did. Baleo says it does.

The system revenue figure comes from Anytime's 2025 FDD (franchise disclosure document, the filing every franchisor gives to buyers). Baleo's numbers are its own, as reported to Franchise Times, and we haven't seen them audited.

Same brand. Same price list. Similar gyms, mostly 5,000 to 6,000 square feet. The gap is 180 members per gym.

What Baleo says it does differently

Nothing fancy. Chere, who runs operations, credits clean, well-kept clubs and personable general managers. A friendly gym, he told Franchise Times, "starts with the general manager."

The founders lead new-hire calls twice a month across the company. They promote from inside. Christopher handles deals and money, and Chere handles the clubs. They joke that Christopher on his own would have 200 clubs that underperform, and Chere on his own would have three great ones.

Christopher also names a weak spot. Many of the brand's systems are about 30 years old. Anytime is redesigning its clubs, and Baleo is pushing the remodels. He pitches the smaller member count as a selling point next to budget gyms with thousands of members per club.

Where this fits

Big franchise systems are more and more run by a few large operators. At Crunch, the top 10 franchisees bring in about 80% of sales, according to S&P's report on its new $800 million bond deal. The same report puts Purpose Brands, which owns Anytime and Orangetheory, at about $600,000 in sales per location.

Baleo is what the buying route looks like inside the smaller box. The other route is to build and standardize, the way Alpha Fit Club is growing from 20 studios to 50. And the owners selling to Baleo are the other half of the story. Even Anytime's Asia master franchise went up for sale in August.

What this means for you

If you own one or two franchised gyms, know what a buyer counts. Baleo talks about members per gym, revenue per gym and clean, well-run clubs. Have those numbers ready long before you want to sell.

If you run several locations, buying may beat building. An open gym comes with members and staff. The catch is that you need a real reason to think you'll run it better. Baleo's reason is people: managers it trains and promotes itself.

If you run an independent studio, the lesson is those 180 members. Baleo got them without a new format or a price cut. It kept the clubs clean, hired well and held on to its managers.

A hundred gyms, almost all of them bought. Baleo's edge is running clubs other people built a little better than they did.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Baleo Fitness?
A large Anytime Fitness franchisee founded by Michael Chere and John Christopher, who met at Columbia Business School. It bought its first two gyms in South Carolina in late 2022 and now runs more than 100 gyms in 15 states.
How did Baleo Fitness grow so fast?
Mostly by buying gyms. About 94% of its gyms were bought from other franchisees, including its first 40. It plans 25 to 30 more a year, about five new builds and the rest acquisitions, and has taken no private equity so far.
How do Baleo's gyms compare with the Anytime Fitness average?
Baleo reports average revenue of about $480,000 per gym and 840 members, against Anytime's 2025 system figures of $446,814 in revenue and 660 members. Baleo's numbers are self-reported to Franchise Times.
