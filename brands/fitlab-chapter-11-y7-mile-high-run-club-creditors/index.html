FitLab Filed for Bankruptcy. Google and Meta Are Listed as Creditors.

The petition lists up to $500 million in debt against $50 million in assets. Two of the creditors are the ad platforms FitLab was still buying members through.

FitLab filed for Chapter 11 on October 26. It runs Y7 Studio, Yoga Vida and Mile High Run Club, and it pulled more than 20 connected companies into the filing with it.

The petition puts the company's debts somewhere between $100 million and $500 million. Its assets are listed far lower, at $10 million to $50 million. And two of the names on the creditor list are Google and Meta.

Why are Google and Meta listed as creditors?

Because FitLab owed them for advertising it had already run. Ad platforms extend credit like any other vendor, so when a company files, they line up alongside the landlords and the equipment suppliers. The detail matters because it shows the business was still buying members while the cash was running out. Paid acquisition is a fixed monthly bill. It does not pause when bookings slow, and it is rarely the first line anyone cuts.

That is the part worth sitting with. If your CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) only works when classes are full, then a soft quarter turns your growth engine into a bill you have already committed to. We looked at how differently operators size that commitment in our breakdown of what the biggest chains actually spend on marketing.

The case sits in the US Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware, numbered 1:26-bk-11681. Law360 and Inc. have both been through the docket.

The filing covers FitLab itself plus Y7, Yoga Vida, Mile High Run Club, RPM Fitness and Ragnar Events. FitLab Fund Management holds 80% of the parent and FitLab LLC holds the rest, so the whole group went in together rather than one brand failing on its own.

Elysian Park Ventures is claiming more than $7 million on a convertible note. Landlord and vendor suits came first, and before any of it a Los Angeles judge had already found FitLab insolvent in a case brought by a painting contractor. That ruling was preliminary, and it was not a bankruptcy judgment, but it was a signal in public months ahead of the filing.

So what happens to members who paid up front?

The petition does not say what happens to the brands. Anyone holding a prepaid class pack becomes an unsecured creditor (someone owed money with no claim on any particular piece of property). That is the back of the queue. Secured lenders get paid first, and whatever is left gets divided among everyone else.

We went through this in detail when True Fitness shut every one of its Singapore gyms overnight and members were left holding more than S$609,000 in classes they had already paid for. The mechanics here are the same.

What Chapter 11 actually means

Chapter 11 lets a company keep trading while it reorganises its debts. Brands get sold, wound down, or carried on under new owners. Ragnar's chief executive has already talked publicly about moving closer to a handover. So some of these names will survive in some form. Y7's studios are not padlocked this morning.

But the shape of the thing should be familiar. An operator grows on cheap money, buys members through paid channels, signs long leases, and then the cost of all three arrives at once. We saw a milder version when 87 Orangetheory studios closed in a year while the royalty stayed charged on revenue, not profit.

What this means for you

Read your own creditor list before anyone else does. If you stopped paying today, who would you owe, and in what order? Most operators can name the landlord and the bank. Far fewer can name how much sits unpaid with the ad platforms, and that number tends to grow quietly.

Then check how much of your member growth is rented. If paid channels bring in most of your new sign-ups, you are paying rent on demand as well as on floor space. The studios that came through the last squeeze were usually the ones whose members arrived through referrals and community, because that supply does not send an invoice.

FitLab had the brands, the partnerships and the footprint. What it ran out of was time. The creditor list tells you where the money was going on the way there.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did FitLab close Y7 Studio and Mile High Run Club?
No. FitLab filed for Chapter 11, which lets a company keep trading while it reorganises its debts. The petition does not say what happens to the individual brands. Some may be sold, some wound down, and some continue under new owners.
Why are Google and Meta creditors in a fitness bankruptcy?
Because FitLab owed them for advertising it had already run. Ad platforms extend credit like any other vendor, so unpaid ad invoices become claims in the bankruptcy alongside landlords and equipment suppliers.
What happens to members who prepaid for classes?
They generally rank as unsecured creditors, which means they sit behind secured lenders and get paid from whatever is left. In past studio collapses, prepaid customers have recovered little or nothing.
